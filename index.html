FACES YOU HAVE MET

A memory aid for names you cannot place. Point at someone, tap their face and give it a name; the next time they are in front of you it says who they are.

All of it stays on this device. What is stored is 128 numbers describing a face, which cannot be turned back into a picture of anyone, and none of it is ever sent anywhere - not even to this app's own endpoint. It is off until you switch it on, and switching it on downloads about eight megabytes of models.

ABOUT

Detection and first-pass labelling run in this browser. Only a cropped region is ever sent for identification, and only when needed. No face data is stored or indexed.

Uncapped, because nothing meters them: the two models on this device, your position and compass, and Wikipedia and Wikidata. Free with a fair-use policy: Open-Meteo for the weather, and OpenStreetMap through Overpass for the streets, cached here for a month so a road you have walked draws with no network at all. On your own Cloudflare account with a daily allowance: the detail tier that gives a make, a model and a price. When that allowance runs out the rest keeps working and names stay generic.

Species are named on this device too, by Google's AIY iNaturalist classifiers - 2,102 plants, 1,021 insects and 965 birds, under Apache 2.0, answering in about 40 milliseconds with no key and no limit. The model for a kind is fetched the first time you point at one. Everything Wikidata knows is added on top: the rank, the family and the conservation status.
